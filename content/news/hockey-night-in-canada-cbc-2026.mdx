**Hockey Night in Canada CBC 2026** coverage ended with the 2025-26 NHL season after CBC and Sportsnet chose not to continue their broadcast partnership. For viewers, the practical change is clear: do not count on CBC's free over-the-air signal or CBC Gem for NHL games in 2026-27. Rogers holds the new national rights, but fans should verify official schedules, packages and regional restrictions before paying for a service.

The CBC-Sportsnet arrangement lasted 12 seasons after Rogers first acquired the national package. The new Rogers agreement runs for another 12 years beginning in 2026-27 and was reported at C$11 billion. That rights change explains the scale of the transition, but it does not tell an individual fan which package is best.

## What Is Confirmed—and What Is Not

Sportsnet and CBC confirmed that NHL games will not return to CBC under the expired partnership. Associated Press reported that CBC retains the *Hockey Night in Canada* trademark and plans other Saturday sports programming. Sportsnet says it will carry all Saturday games moving forward.

The NHL released its full 2026-27 schedule on July 16. The regular season expands to 84 games per team, starts September 29 and ends April 10. That schedule tells you **when** teams play; official broadcast listings still determine **where** each game is available.

What is not safe to assume is that every national, regional and French-language game will appear in one identical package. Rights can differ by team, territory, language and game type. Existing arrangements may also expire or change.

Before the puck drops, verify these details in the broadcaster's current listings:

- Your team's national and regional games.
- Whether your postal region creates a restriction.
- The package tier required for those games.
- Supported TVs, streaming devices and concurrent streams.
- Monthly, annual, cancellation and trial terms.

Avoid relying on an old comparison page or social-media screenshot for current prices.

## A Five-Minute NHL Subscription Audit

First, list the games you actually watch: one local team, national Saturday games, playoffs, or every out-of-market game. Next, open your internet, mobile and television bills. Some bundles already include a sports channel, while others duplicate a standalone subscription.

Then compare the full-season cost, not a promotional first month. A monthly plan can be useful for testing service quality and cancelling outside hockey season. An annual plan may cost less only if it includes the exact games you need and you will use it long enough.

## Streaming Quality Still Depends on Your Connection

Changing rights does not change the basics of live-stream reliability. Use Ethernet where practical, place Wi-Fi equipment away from interference, update the viewing app and test on the same device you will use on game night.
