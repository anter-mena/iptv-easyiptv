Quebecor Q2 2026 results, released August 6, delivered growth across every headline measure — revenue up **4.3%**, net income up **24.4%**, free cash flow up **11.7%**, and a **12.5% dividend increase**. But the most instructive number for anyone watching Canadian television sat in the media segment, where **TVA Sports gained 3.0 percentage points of market share** on the back of a single playoff run.

## The Quebecor Q2 2026 headline numbers

| Measure | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidated revenues | **$1.44B** | +$59.8M (+4.3%) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $627.4M | +$22.3M (+3.7%) |
| Net income attributable to shareholders | **$270.9M** ($1.21/share) | +$53.2M (+24.4%) |
| Free cash flows | $418.7M | +$43.8M (+11.7%) |
| Quarterly dividend | **$0.45/share** | **+12.5%** |

Excluding a stock-based compensation impact, adjusted EBITDA rose **9.8%**.

> "Free cash flows increased by 11.7%, revenues by 4.3% and adjusted EBITDA by 3.7%." — **Pierre Karl Péladeau, President and Chief Executive Officer, Quebecor**

## Quebecor Q2 2026 telecommunications: mobile did the work

Videotron's telecommunications segment reported revenues of **$1.234 billion, up 4.0%**, with adjusted EBITDA of **$641.7 million, up 5.3%**.

The growth split is worth noting:

- **Mobile services: +$40.2 million (+9.2%)** — the clear driver.
- **Internet access: +$9.6 million (+3.1%)** — steady.
- **Mobile subscriber connections: +53,200 net (+1.2%).**
- **Mobile ARPU: +2.5%**, a third consecutive quarterly increase.

Rising ARPU alongside subscriber growth is an unusual combination in Canadian wireless, where the two have frequently moved in opposite directions during price competition.

Quebecor also increased its stake in **Etiya to 70%** on April 21, 2026, taking majority control of the AI-powered business support systems provider it uses across Videotron, Freedom Mobile and Fizz.

## The sports story inside Quebecor Q2 2026

The media segment produced the most dramatic swing in Quebecor Q2 2026. **TVA Group's adjusted EBITDA rose to $23.3 million — an increase of $21.6 million**, meaning the segment went from roughly break-even to solidly profitable in twelve months.

The driver was specific and time-limited: **TVA Sports reached 8.6% market share, up 3.0 percentage points**, on NHL playoff coverage and Montreal Canadiens postseason programming. Quebecor reported those games drew **up to two million viewers** with **nearly 50% market share** at peak.

Two observations follow.

**Live sports remains the single most powerful driver of television audiences in Canada.** A specialty channel moved three points of share — an enormous shift in broadcast terms — on one team's playoff run.

**That kind of gain is inherently seasonal.** A postseason ends. The comparison in future quarters will be against this elevated base, and Quebecor did not suggest otherwise.

## Why this matters for Canadian viewers

The read-across for households is about **where the value in a television subscription is concentrating**.

1. **Sports rights keep getting more valuable** because they deliver the audiences nothing else does. That value flows into what packages carrying those channels cost.
2. **Regional rights fragment the picture.** A Canadiens run lifts TVA Sports specifically. Following a particular team increasingly determines which service you need — not which is best overall.
3. **The wireless-first pattern continues.** Quebecor's growth came from mobile, not television. Across the industry, TV is no longer the segment driving telecom results.

That last point connects to a broader shift we have tracked this earnings season. **BCE reported video subscribers up 3.1%** — on a metric it redefined effective January 1, 2026 to include bundled streaming subscribers. **TELUS discontinued TV and video subscriber reporting** entirely from the same date. **Rogers shed 111,000 TV subscribers** in the first half of 2026.

Four carriers, four different ways of describing the same declining product. Quebecor's Q2 release led with mobile metrics rather than television subscriber counts — consistent with that pattern, though the company made no statement about changing how it reports.

## Capital returns

The board raised the quarterly dividend **12.5%, from $0.40 to $0.45 per share**, and renewed the share repurchase programme through **August 14, 2027**, with authorisation for up to 1,000,000 Class A Shares and 7,000,000 Class B Shares.

A double-digit dividend increase alongside an 11.7% rise in free cash flow is a straightforwardly confident set of decisions — and a notable contrast with other parts of the Canadian corporate landscape this month.

## Quebecor Q2 2026 and the four-carrier TV picture

Put this quarter beside the three other major Canadian carriers and a pattern emerges that no single release makes obvious.

| Carrier | How it described TV in 2026 |
|---|---|
| **BCE** | Video subscribers **+3.1%** — on a metric **redefined** 1 Jan 2026 to include bundled streaming |
| **TELUS** | **Discontinued** TV/video subscriber reporting effective 1 Jan 2026 |
| **Rogers** | Still reporting — **shed 111,000** TV subscribers in H1 2026 |
| **Quebecor** | Led with mobile metrics; growth came from wireless and sports |

![Four Canadian carriers reporting television differently, the context around Quebecor Q2 2026](https://images.pexels.com/photos/21792101/pexels-photo-21792101.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

The two carriers that changed or dropped the measure did so in the same January. The one that kept reporting a conventional count reported losses. Quebecor made no statement about changing its reporting, and none should be inferred — but its growth story was unambiguously mobile and sports, not television subscriptions.

For viewers, that is the practical signal: the product your provider is investing in is increasingly not the TV package. Our coverage of [BCE's redefined video metric](/news/bce-q2-2026-tv-subscribers) explains how much that reporting change actually moved.

## Key takeaways

- **Quebecor Q2 2026 revenues rose 4.3%** to $1.44 billion; net income up **24.4%** to $270.9 million.
- **Free cash flows rose 11.7%** to $418.7 million; the dividend was raised **12.5%**.
- **Videotron added 53,200 mobile connections**; ARPU up 2.5%, a third straight quarterly rise.
- **TVA Sports gained 3.0 points of share to 8.6%**, drawing up to two million viewers on Canadiens playoff games.
- **Media segment adjusted EBITDA rose $21.6 million** to $23.3 million.
- **Growth came from mobile and sports**, not from television subscriptions.

## The bottom line

Quebecor Q2 2026 was a strong quarter built on two engines: wireless subscriber and ARPU growth, and an exceptional but seasonal sports performance. The dividend increase and buyback renewal suggest management views the underlying momentum as durable.

For viewers, the lesson is the one this earnings season keeps repeating. **Television is no longer where the growth is — sports is.** A single playoff run swung an entire media segment from break-even to $23.3 million, which explains rather neatly why live rights keep getting more expensive, and why what you pay for TV increasingly depends on which games you want to watch.
