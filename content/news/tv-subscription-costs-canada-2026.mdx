**TV subscription costs Canada 2026** are under a harder light this week. The Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) released figures on 10 August showing **37,523 consumer insolvency filings** in the second quarter — the most in any quarter since 2009 — with **13,254 filings in June alone**, up 11.5% year over year. Recurring subscriptions are among the first things households review under that kind of pressure, and television is usually near the top of the list. Before cancelling anything, it is worth knowing which rules protect you and which ones do not apply.

None of this is a television story on its own. It becomes one because subscription spending is discretionary, visible on a bank statement, and easy to act on — which is exactly why the rules around cancelling deserve to be better known than they are.

## Five Rules Behind TV Subscription Costs Canada 2026

**1. No notice period can be imposed.** Under the CRTC's Television Service Provider (TVSP) Code, a provider may not require 30 days' notice to cancel. Cancellation is effective the day you request it, or a later date you nominate.

**2. Price increases require at least 30 days' notice.** If your bill is going up, you are entitled to advance warning — which is also your window to shop alternatives.

**3. Early-cancellation charges must be disclosed upfront.** Any such formula must appear in your written agreement and in the Critical Information Summary provided when you signed.

**4. The 2026 fee ban does not cover television.** This is the detail most commonly got wrong. The CRTC's decision in force since 12 June 2026 prohibits activation, modification and early-cancellation fees — but under the **Wireless Code and the Internet Code**. Television sits under the separate TVSP Code and was not included.

**5. Order of operations matters.** Because internet and TV are governed by different codes, cancelling a bundle can trigger different outcomes on each component. Confirm what happens to your internet pricing before you cancel the television portion.

## Context: A Long Shift Already Underway

Rising TV subscription costs Canada 2026 households face are landing on a market that has been contracting for years. By the end of 2025, roughly **48.5% of Canadian households had no cable, satellite or telecom TV subscription**, up from about 46% a year earlier.

The industry's own reporting has grown thinner alongside it. TELUS discontinued TV and video subscriber reporting effective 1 January 2026, and Bell redefined its video metric on the same date. Rogers, which still reports, shed roughly **111,000 television subscribers in the first half of 2026**. Our earlier coverage of [cord-cutting in Canada](/news/canada-cord-cutting-pay-tv-2026) tracked the same trend.

Meanwhile the rulebook that shapes TV subscription costs Canada 2026 is being rewritten. CRTC consultation **2026-134** proposes merging four existing codes — the Deposit and Disconnection Code, the TVSP Code, the Wireless Code and the Internet Code — into a single Consumer Protection Code. A public hearing is scheduled for **30 November 2026** in Gatineau.

## Why This Matters

An honest note belongs here. Trimming a television subscription does not resolve debt that has reached insolvency levels — the arithmetic simply does not work at that scale. Anyone in that position should speak to a **Licensed Insolvency Trustee**, whose initial consultation is free and who is the only professional able to file a consumer proposal or bankruptcy in Canada. Non-profit credit counselling agencies also help at little or no cost.

What the rules above do change is whether a household reviewing TV subscription costs Canada 2026 keeps its options open or pays for the privilege of leaving. Knowing that no notice period applies, that price rises require 30 days' warning, and that the new fee ban stops short of television is worth real money at the moment of cancelling.
