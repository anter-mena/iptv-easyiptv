**Streaming closed captioning Canada** requirements are expanding under a new CRTC policy covering registered audio-visual online services. The framework sets caption obligations for new original programs, previously captioned television shows, live streams and large portions of streaming catalogues. For viewers, the useful question is not only when the rules take effect, but how to identify and report a caption failure.

> **Verified July 16, 2026:** This explainer uses CRTC policy 2026-98 and the regulator's accessibility release. The CRTC is implementing conditions through orders, so final order wording and provider implementation should be monitored.

## Streaming Closed Captioning Canada: Seven Key Rules

| Requirement | Coverage | Timeline or limit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. New original pre-recorded programs | 100% in English or French | Short implementation period set by the policy |
| 2. Original live programs | 100% in English or French | Includes original live news or sports |
| 3. Previously captioned TV programs | Captions must carry over online | Applies when shown online by covered services |
| 4. Third-party live programs | Include captions supplied by the third party | Service is encouraged to improve wider access |
| 5. Programme catalogue | 80% captioned | By May 27, 2030 |
| 6. Full catalogue | 100% captioned | By May 26, 2031 |
| 7. Reporting and complaints | Annual progress and complaint reporting | Supports public monitoring |

The [CRTC policy](https://www.crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2026/2026-98.htm) says captions should include spoken dialogue and relevant audio context, such as the speaker, music and important sound effects.

## 1. New Original Programs Must Be Captioned

Covered online streaming services must provide closed captions for all new original pre-recorded English- and French-language programs. Original programming is content the service produces, co-produces or commissions under the policy's definitions.

This is a strong requirement because the service has more control over the production workflow. Captions can be planned, edited and quality-checked before release rather than added years later.

The rule is not limited to major dramas. It can cover eligible original documentaries, reality programs, children's shows and other audio-visual content.

Viewers should still check the title details. "Subtitles" and "closed captions" may be labelled differently by each app.

## 2. Original Live Streams Are Included

The **streaming closed captioning Canada** policy also requires captions for covered services' original English- and French-language live programs.

Live captioning is more difficult than pre-recorded captioning because accuracy, speaker identification and timing must be handled immediately. The CRTC considered these challenges but concluded that services controlling original live programming can plan caption access by design.

For viewers, this matters for original live news, entertainment and sports productions. A caption delay of several seconds may occur, but excessive lag or missing context can still reduce access.

Record the exact time of a live problem. That makes a complaint more useful than saying only "captions were bad."

## 3. Television Captions Should Follow the Program Online

When an English- or French-language program was previously broadcast on traditional television with captions, a covered streaming service must provide those captions when it makes the program available online.

This addresses a common viewer frustration: a show is accessible on television but loses captions inside an app or on-demand library.

If the same episode has captions on one platform but not another, document:

- program and episode title;
- season and episode number;
- streaming service;
- device and app version;
- whether the television version was captioned;
- screenshot of the missing caption menu.

The difference may be a metadata, licensing or app-delivery problem rather than the absence of a caption file.

## 4. Third-Party Live Content Has a More Limited Rule

For non-original live programming, the service must include captions when the third-party supplier provides them. The CRTC stopped short of requiring a service to create captions for every live feed it does not control.

This distinction is important for sports packages containing several simultaneous feeds. The main broadcast may include captions while an alternate camera or international feed does not.

The CRTC encourages services to ensure broader third-party live captioning and requires annual reporting on progress. Viewers should not assume that every secondary feed is already guaranteed.

## 5. Catalogues Will Phase Toward 100%

Older streaming libraries may include thousands of hours of content produced under different contracts and technical standards. The CRTC therefore adopted a phased approach:

- at least 80% of the catalogue by May 27, 2030;
- 100% by May 26, 2031.

The regulator encourages services to caption the most frequently watched programs first. Annual reporting is intended to show progress.

This phase-in does not mean services should wait until 2030 to improve. It establishes minimum milestones while allowing caption resources, rights and technology to develop.

## 6. Quality and Controls Matter

Captions are not useful merely because text appears. Quality includes:

- accuracy;
- completeness;
- readable placement;
- reasonable synchronization;
- speaker identification;
- descriptions of relevant audio;
- colour, size and background customization where available.

The CRTC expects services to make captions easy to activate and to minimize the number of steps. A viewer should not need to leave the program and search several account menus.

The policy also expects providers to work toward customization. This can be particularly important for DeafBlind viewers and people who require higher contrast or larger text.

If captions overlap on-screen information, try changing size or background settings before reporting the problem. If placement cannot be adjusted, include that detail in the report.

## 7. Complaints and Annual Reports Create Accountability

Covered services must report information about caption quality standards, third-party efforts, complaints, catalogue progress and customization.

The CRTC expects services to document caption errors and, where possible, retain unavailable programs for 30 days while a complaint is active. It also encourages an in-player flag or reporting button.

Use the provider's accessibility channel first when practical. If the problem remains unresolved, file a CRTC complaint with:

1. account and service name;
2. title, episode and timestamp;
3. device, operating system and app version;
4. screenshot or short description of the error;
5. steps already attempted;
6. provider case number and response;
7. why the problem creates an accessibility barrier.

A detailed report is easier to investigate and can help identify repeated platform failures.

## Who Is Covered and Who Is Not

The **streaming closed captioning Canada** policy applies to audio-visual online streaming undertakings required to register under the CRTC Registration Regulations.

It does not generally apply to:

- individual social-media creators on social-media services;
- text-focused websites that happen to contain occasional clips;
- third-language programs under this English- and French-language policy;
- every one-time online video rental in the same way;
- small exempt services outside the registration scope.

An IPTV app is not automatically covered simply because it uses internet delivery. The provider's regulatory status and role in offering the programming matter.

## A Five-Minute Caption Troubleshooting Guide

When captions are missing:

1. open the in-player audio and subtitle menu;
2. confirm the selected language and caption track;
3. restart the program and app;
4. update the app and streaming device;
5. test the same title on another supported device;
6. turn off conflicting device-level subtitle settings;
7. report the issue with the exact episode and time.

If captions work on a phone but not a set-top box, the problem may be device-specific. If they fail on every device, it may be tied to the title or account region.

## Bottom Line

The new **streaming closed captioning Canada** framework moves registered online video services toward consistent captioning of new originals, live programming, previously captioned TV shows and eventually complete catalogues.

The strongest viewer benefit will depend on implementation quality. Learn where the accessibility controls are, record specific failures and use provider and CRTC complaint channels when captions are missing, inaccurate or difficult to activate.
