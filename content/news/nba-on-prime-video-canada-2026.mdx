**NBA on Prime Video Canada** arrives in October 2026, and it is one of the clearest signs yet that live sports keep moving to streaming — and behind more paywalls. As reported by MobileSyrup from Amazon Upfront 2026 in Toronto, the Canadian package includes 66 regular-season games, the Emirates NBA Cup 2026, playoff coverage and more, all as part of an 11-year global NBA deal. The catch for cord-cutters: it does not carry every game, so following your team still means juggling more than one service.

## Quick answer: what NBA on Prime Video Canada includes

The deal is broad but deliberately not complete. Here is what the Canadian package covers, according to MobileSyrup's reporting from the Amazon upfront:

| NBA on Prime Video Canada (from Oct 2026) | Included? |
|---|---|
| 66 regular-season games | Yes |
| Emirates NBA Cup 2026 | Yes |
| All six 2027 Play-In Tournament games | Yes |
| Exclusive first- and second-round playoff coverage | Yes |
| Exclusive Conference Finals (6 of 11 years, from 2027) | Yes |
| Every regular-season game | No — needs Sportsnet / TSN |
| Access without a Prime membership | No — Prime required |

So the exclusives are real — a Conference Finals series only on Prime Video in six of the 11 years is a genuine draw — but a full-season habit will need more than one login.

## What Prime Video Canada won't cover

The most important line for budget-conscious fans is the one about coverage. NBA on Prime Video Canada does **not** include every NBA game. To watch the rest of the schedule, viewers will still need Sportsnet and/or TSN, both offered as Prime Video Channels. And all of it sits behind a Prime membership, which is a separate cost from any sports add-on.

That is the pattern across live sports right now: rights get split among more services, and each split adds another subscription. If you follow one team closely, the honest question is how many of these you are willing to stack — and at what monthly total.

## The rising cost of following your team

More live sport on streaming is convenient, but it is not automatically cheaper. Each rights deal that lands on a new platform can mean another bill, and those bills are climbing: a MobileSyrup report found Canadian streaming prices rose an average of 7% in 2025 across the leading platforms, with steeper increases expected in 2026.

For a basketball fan, that can mean Prime plus a Sportsnet or TSN add-on just to see most of a season — the same fragmentation hockey fans already know well. ## How cord-cutters can plan for it

Before adding another app, do the math the way you would for any subscription:

- **Count the real cost.** Prime membership plus any Sportsnet or TSN channel you need — not just the headline "NBA on Prime Video" line.
- **Match it to your team.** Check how many of your team's games actually fall in Prime's 66-game slate versus Sportsnet or TSN before you commit.
- **Rotate around the calendar.** Playoffs and the NBA Cup are where the exclusives concentrate, so you may not need every service year-round.

## Bottom line

**NBA on Prime Video Canada** is a meaningful win for fans who want big games, exclusive playoff rounds and the NBA Cup without a cable box — starting October 2026 as part of an 11-year deal. As live sport keeps splitting across platforms, the smart play is to price the whole picture before you subscribe, not just the launch headline.
