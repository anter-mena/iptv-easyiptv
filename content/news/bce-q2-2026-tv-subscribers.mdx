BCE Q2 2026 delivered something no other major Canadian carrier reported this earnings season: a **growing** television subscriber base. Bell finished the quarter with 2,164,083 video subscribers, up 3.1% from a year earlier — at a moment when Rogers is shedding TV customers and TELUS has stopped publishing the number altogether. The results, released on August 6, 2026, are worth reading carefully, because the growth and the definition of what is being counted changed in the same year.

## The BCE Q2 2026 headline numbers

BCE reported operating revenue of **$6,176 million**, up 1.5% year over year, with service revenue of $5.5 billion rising 4.3%.

| Measure | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Operating revenue | $6,176M | +1.5% |
| Net earnings | $629M | −2.3% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $2,702M | +1.0% |
| Adjusted EPS | $0.65 | +3.2% |
| Free cash flow | $1,042M | −9.5% |
| Video subscribers | 2,164,083 | +3.1% |
| Total high-speed internet subscribers | 4,911,422 | +7.3% |
| Postpaid mobile phone net additions | 41,594 | churn 1.02% |

Profit attributable to common shareholders was **$558 million, or 60 cents per diluted share**, down from $579 million and 63 cents a year earlier, according to The Canadian Press summary of the filing.

The quarterly dividend was held at **$0.4375 per common share**, payable October 15, 2026 to shareholders of record on September 15, 2026 — an annualized $1.75. BCE reaffirmed 2026 guidance of 1% to 5% revenue growth, 0% to 4% adjusted EBITDA growth, capital intensity near 20%, and free cash flow of $2,100 million to $2,300 million.

Fibre carried the operational story. Bell added **54,883 FTTH internet subscribers** — 45,271 in Canada and 9,612 in the United States — a 14.5% year-over-year increase that drove **14.2% internet revenue growth**. Postpaid mobile churn improved to **1.02%**, which BCE described as its lowest quarterly level in three years.

> "Fibre continues to drive growth with nearly 55,000 FTTH Internet subscriber additions, contributing to 14.2% Internet revenue growth." — **Mirko Bibic, President and CEO, BCE**

## BCE Q2 2026: the video subscriber rule that changed

This is the part of BCE Q2 2026 that deserves attention from anyone tracking Canadian television.

Effective **January 1, 2026**, BCE updated the definition of an IPTV subscriber to include **bundled streaming service subscribers**, reported under a new *video subscriber* metric. In the company's own words from its Q1 2026 release:

> "To be classified as a bundled streaming service subscriber, a customer must subscribe to a package that includes at least one third-party streaming service and one streaming service offered by BCE (comprised of duos, trios and quad packages including Crave, TSN, Netflix and Disney+) where BCE has a direct customer relationship."

BCE also stated that **previously reported 2025 subscribers and net activations have been restated for comparability**. That matters, and it should be said plainly: this was a disclosed change with restated prior periods, not a hidden one. The year-over-year comparison inside BCE's own filing is internally consistent.

What it is not is a like-for-like comparison with the number the industry reported before 2026, or with what most readers picture when they hear "TV subscribers." A household that buys an internet package with Netflix and Crave attached — and never subscribes to a traditional channel line-up — can now sit inside the video subscriber count.

## What BCE Q2 2026 says next to Rogers, TELUS and Cogeco

Read BCE Q2 2026 across the sector and a pattern emerges — not of a single trend, but of four companies answering the same awkward question in four different ways.

![A quarterly earnings table on a laptop beside a television remote, comparing BCE Q2 2026 subscriber metrics across Canadian carriers](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4009407/pexels-photo-4009407.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

| Company | What it reported for TV/video in 2026 |
|---|---|
| **BCE** | Video subscribers **up 3.1%** to 2,164,083 — on a metric broadened January 1, 2026 |
| **TELUS** | **Discontinued** TV and video subscriber reporting effective January 1, 2026 |
| **Rogers** | Still reporting — **shed 111,000** TV subscribers in the first half of 2026 |
| **Cogeco** | Cited an **overall decline** in video and wireline phone subscribers as customers move to internet-only |

Two of the four changed or dropped the measure in the same January. The two that kept reporting a conventional count both reported losses. That is the context in which a 3.1% gain should be read — and it is the reason we covered [the number TELUS stopped publishing](/news/telus-q2-2026-tv-subscribers) and [the 111,000 subscribers Rogers lost](/news/rogers-tv-subscriber-losses-q2-2026) as separate stories.

Bell Media, meanwhile, had a genuinely strong quarter on content. Revenue rose **8.9% to $918 million**, with advertising up 5.3% and subscriber revenue up 6.7%, driven largely by the FIFA World Cup. Live coverage reached **30.5 million unique Canadian viewers**, and the Spain–Argentina final on July 19 averaged **6.4 million viewers** — the highest Canadian World Cup audience on record. Crave subscriptions rose **23% to 5.07 million**.

Bell Media's adjusted EBITDA rose 3.8% to $244 million, though margin slipped to 26.6% from 27.9% on World Cup and content costs — a reminder that record audiences are bought, not found.

## Why BCE Q2 2026 matters to your TV bill

Four practical implications of BCE Q2 2026 for Canadian viewers.

**1. "TV subscribers" is no longer a comparable industry statistic.** With one carrier broadening the definition, one discontinuing it and two reporting declines, cross-company comparisons in 2026 are not measuring the same thing. Treat any "Canadians are/aren't cutting the cord" headline built on carrier subscriber counts with care.

**2. The bundle is the strategy.** A metric that counts streaming-plus-connectivity packages is aligned with where the revenue is: internet revenue grew 14.2%, Crave grew 23%. Expect bundles to be the shape of most offers you see.

**3. Bundles are not automatically cheaper.** A duo or trio can genuinely undercut buying each service separately — or quietly commit you to services you would not have chosen. The arithmetic is worth doing per household, which is what our [IPTV versus cable comparison](/blog/iptv-vs-cable-canada) walks through.

**4. Nothing here announces a price change.** BCE published no pricing decision in this release, and none should be inferred from it.

For broader context on how many Canadian households have already left traditional TV, see our coverage of [Canada's cord-cutting picture](/news/canada-cord-cutting-pay-tv-2026).

## Timeline: how Canada's TV subscriber number fractured

- **January 1, 2026** — BCE broadens its IPTV definition into a new *video subscriber* metric including bundled streaming subscribers; 2025 figures restated.
- **January 1, 2026** — TELUS revises its reporting structure; TV and video subscriber data discontinued.
- **First half 2026** — Rogers reports a loss of 111,000 TV subscribers.
- **July 31, 2026** — TELUS Q2 arrives with no TV subscriber figure.
- **August 6, 2026** — BCE Q2 2026 reports video subscribers up 3.1% to 2,164,083, the only growth figure of the four.
- **August 6, 2026** — Cogeco reports continued video subscriber decline as customers shift to internet-only.

## BCE Q2 2026: key takeaways

- **BCE Q2 2026 revenue** rose 1.5% to $6,176 million; net earnings fell 2.3% to $629 million.
- **Video subscribers reached 2,164,083, up 3.1%** — on a metric redefined January 1, 2026.
- The new definition counts **bundled streaming packages** including Crave, TSN, Netflix and Disney+; **2025 figures were restated**.
- **Fibre and mobility were strong**: 54,883 FTTH additions, 14.2% internet revenue growth, churn at a three-year low of 1.02%.
- **Bell Media rose 8.9%** on a record World Cup — 30.5 million unique viewers; Crave up 23% to 5.07 million.
- **The dividend held** at $0.4375 quarterly; 2026 guidance reaffirmed.

## The bottom line

Taken as a whole, BCE Q2 2026 is a solid operational quarter — fibre growth, improving churn, a record sports audience and a maintained dividend — reported alongside a television metric that no longer means what it meant a year ago. Both things are true, and BCE disclosed the change and restated its comparatives, which is the right way to make one.

For viewers, the useful takeaway is narrower than the headline: Bell is growing the *bundle*, and the bundle is now what gets counted as television. Whether that is good value depends entirely on whether you would have bought the streaming services anyway — a question no subscriber count can answer for you.
