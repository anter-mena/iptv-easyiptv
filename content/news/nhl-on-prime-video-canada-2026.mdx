**NHL on Prime Video Canada** is now official: Amazon's Prime Video will exclusively carry Wednesday-night national NHL regular-season games under a 12-year licensing deal with Rogers, in English and French. It is a genuine convenience — those games come included with a Prime membership at no extra cost — but it also pushes Canadian sports viewing further down the road of fragmentation, where following your favourite teams means juggling, and paying for, more services than ever.

## The News: NHL on Prime Video Canada, Explained

According to [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/amazon-prime-video-wednesday-nhl-canada-9.7287491), Prime Video will exclusively broadcast Wednesday-night national NHL games in Canada, starting September 30, as part of a 12-year agreement with Rogers. The package covers at least 26 national regular-season games plus a handful of playoff series, and it is available to Prime members at no additional charge.

Two things are worth keeping straight. First, this is not Amazon taking over the league — Rogers-owned Sportsnet keeps the majority of national games and remains the NHL's primary home in Canada. Second, it is an expansion rather than a debut: Prime Video already carried Monday-night games for the past two seasons, and NHL on Prime Video Canada now adds the busier Wednesday window on top of that.

So the practical shift is modest but telling. One more night of hockey moves to one more app — a small change on its own, and a clear signal of where live sport in Canada is heading.

## The Bigger Picture: Sports Streaming Is Splintering

The reason NHL on Prime Video Canada matters beyond hockey is that it fits a pattern. Rights that once sat on one or two channels are now scattered across streamers, and each split adds another login and another bill. Here is who carries what across the major leagues right now:

| League | Where to watch in Canada | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| NHL (national) | Sportsnet / Sportsnet+ for most games; Prime Video for Wednesday-night games | Sportsnet+ $29.99/mo; Prime games included with a Prime membership |
| NBA | Prime Video (66 games from Oct 2026); rest on Sportsnet / TSN | Included with Prime; add Sportsnet/TSN for the full slate |
| NFL | DAZN | About $24.99/mo |
| CFL | TSN (Bell Media) for 60 of 81 games + Grey Cup; DAZN for a Saturday game weekly from 2027 | TSN via cable/streaming; separate DAZN subscription |

No single subscription carries all of it. A household that follows hockey and one other sport can quickly stack Prime, Sportsnet+ and DAZN just to see the games it cares about. We broke down the basketball side of this in our [NBA on Prime Video Canada 2026](/news/nba-on-prime-video-canada-2026) report — same platform, same catch: it is only ever part of the schedule.

## How We Got Here

The fragmentation did not start with Prime Video. Rogers holds national NHL rights under a 12-year deal reported at roughly $11 billion, and it is now sub-licensing a weekly window to Amazon rather than airing everything itself. At the same time, CBC no longer airs *Hockey Night in Canada*, so the one free national route to NHL games has closed. We covered that shift, and the jump in Sportsnet+ to $29.99 a month, in [watching NHL cord-cutting in Canada 2026](/news/watch-nhl-cord-cutting-canada-2026).

Football is splintering the same way. As [CBC Sports reported](https://www.cbc.ca/sports/football/cfl/cfl-broadcast-deal-may-2026-9.7214764), the CFL signed a six-year broadcast deal with Bell Media, DAZN and YouTube worth a reported $500 million. TSN will carry 60 of 81 regular-season games plus the Grey Cup, while DAZN takes an exclusive Saturday-night game each week beginning in 2027. Different league, identical trend: the schedule gets sliced, and a streamer picks up the pieces.

Put NHL on Prime Video Canada beside those deals and the direction is obvious. Live sport is the anchor that kept people paying for cable, and now it is the same anchor pulling cord-cutters into a growing pile of streaming subscriptions.

## Why It Matters for Cord-Cutters

The convenience of NHL on Prime Video Canada is real, but so is the arithmetic. Every rights deal that lands on a new platform is another monthly charge, and the totals add up faster than any single "included with your membership" headline suggests.

Play out a modest example. A fan who wants full national hockey pays for Sportsnet+ at $29.99 a month, gets the Wednesday games through a Prime membership they may already have, and adds DAZN at about $24.99 a month to follow the NFL. That is three services and roughly $55 a month before a single entertainment app — and before the CFL's Saturday DAZN window arrives in 2027. ## Key Takeaways

- **Prime Video gets Wednesday-night national NHL games** in English and French from September 30, under a 12-year deal with Rogers, included with a Prime membership.
- **Sportsnet is still the primary home.** Prime carries one window plus select playoff series; the majority of national games stay on Sportsnet and Sportsnet+ ($29.99/mo).
- **Sports viewing is fragmenting fast:** the NFL is on DAZN (about $24.99/mo), the NBA is coming to Prime, and the CFL is split across TSN, DAZN and YouTube.
- **The real cost is the stack, not the headline.** Following two leagues can mean three or more paid services before any entertainment app.
- **Cord-cutters win by pricing the whole picture** — checking which service carries their teams, rotating around the calendar, and comparing stacked apps against one all-in-one plan.

## The Bottom Line

**NHL on Prime Video Canada** is a small, sensible win — one more night of hockey on an app millions already pay for. But it lands in a season where the NHL, NFL, NBA and CFL are all scattering across different streamers, and that scattering is the real story. Following your teams in 2026 is less about any single subscription and more about managing a growing list of them. The smart move is the unglamorous one: add up the whole bill, confirm what each service actually carries, and decide whether stitching together several apps still beats one tidy, flat-priced plan.
