A **Rogers internet price increase** of **$7 per month plus taxes** takes effect on the first bill issued on or after **August 18, 2026**, affecting certain home internet packages. Customers were notified through billing notices. It is the company's **second** home internet increase of 2026, and it lands on the single line item every streaming household pays regardless of which services it subscribes to.

*Reported August 2, 2026, based on reporting published July 6, 2026 and customer billing notices. Affected plans vary by account — check your own notice. This is news reporting and general information.*

## The 5 key facts

| # | Fact |
|---|---|
| 1 | **$7 per month plus taxes** on certain home internet packages |
| 2 | Effective on the **first bill on or after August 18, 2026** |
| 3 | Customers notified by **billing notice**, not a public announcement |
| 4 | **Second** Rogers home internet increase of 2026 |
| 5 | **Promotional discounts and guaranteed rate periods are protected** until they expire |

In its notice, Rogers told customers: *"We continually invest to bring you our best-connected experience in Canada. To help support these investments, we will be increasing the monthly fee."*

## What the Rogers internet price increase means in practice

The Rogers internet price increase works out to **$84 a year before tax** — roughly the cost of seven months of an ad-supported streaming tier. For a household that cut cable specifically to save money, that is a meaningful share of the saving quietly returning.

It is also not the first increase this year. Earlier in 2026, some customers on two-year terms reported increases in the **$7 to $10** range; one widely cited example involved a discounted rate moving from **$52 to $62** a month on a high-speed plan. Households that have been on the same account for a while may therefore be absorbing a second or third adjustment rather than a first.

Two details are worth checking on your own account rather than assuming:

- **Whether your plan is affected.** The increase applies to certain packages, not all of them, and the affected plans were not published as a single public list. Your billing notice is the authoritative source.
- **Whether you are inside a protected period.** Active promotional discounts and guaranteed rate periods remain protected until expiry — which also means the increase may simply arrive later, when that period ends.

## Background: why a Rogers internet price increase draws so many complaints

Price changes on existing accounts are not a niche irritation. They are the single largest source of telecom grievance in the country.

The Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS) accepted a record **23,647 complaints** in its year ending July 31, 2025 — a 17% rise — with **billing issues up 16%**, the highest in five years, and **TV service issues up 44%**. Rogers, which now includes Shaw, accounted for the largest share of accepted complaints at **27%**, ahead of TELUS at 21% and Bell at 17%.

The trend has continued. The CCTS mid-year report covering **August 1, 2025 to January 31, 2026** recorded **19,157** accepted complaints, a **61% increase**, with billing again the leading concern. The agency reported that **88%** of concluded complaints were successfully resolved.

That resolution rate is the useful part for consumers: complaints that get escalated properly tend to get fixed.

## Why this matters for streaming households

The cord-cutting arithmetic only works if the connection underneath it stays affordable. Every dollar added to a home internet bill is a dollar subtracted from the saving generated by dropping a TV package — and unlike a streaming subscription, you cannot cancel the internet.

Three things worth doing this month:

- **Call before you accept.** Ask directly what retention or loyalty pricing is available. Providers frequently apply a discount rather than lose a subscriber, and the call costs nothing.
- **Audit what the connection actually needs to carry.** Most households over-buy speed. For streaming, sustained stability matters more than the headline number — our guide on [how much data IPTV uses](/blog/how-much-data-does-iptv-use) helps size a plan honestly.
- **Escalate properly if the change was mishandled.** Contact the provider first; if unresolved, the CCTS is the formal route, and billing complaints — including disputes over a Rogers internet price increase or any provider's mid-term change — are its largest category.

The wider context is competitive. Independent providers are the main source of downward pressure on retail internet pricing in Canada, and a coalition of eight of them has asked the CRTC to review the wholesale fibre rates they pay incumbents — arguing those rates make undercutting impossible. We covered that proceeding in our report on [wholesale fibre rates under CRTC review](/news/wholesale-fibre-rates-canada). Until it is resolved, increases like this one face limited competitive resistance.
