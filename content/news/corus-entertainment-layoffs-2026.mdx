## Brief summary

**Corus Entertainment layoffs** confirmed on **August 19, 2026** affected roles at Global BC, Global National, News 640 Toronto and the company's talk radio operations. Corus called them "a small number of changes" and gave no headcount. The number that explains them is elsewhere: television **subscriber revenue fell 13%** and **advertising revenue fell 20%** in the company's most recent quarter.

## Introduction

Canadian broadcast trade outlet Broadcast Dialogue reported on August 19 that Corus Entertainment had confirmed a further round of job reductions. The company issued a short statement rather than a figure.

Taken alone, an unquantified round of cuts at one broadcaster is a modest story. Set against the company's own quarterly filings, it is a clear illustration of what cord-cutting looks like from the other side of the screen.

## The main story: what the Corus Entertainment layoffs cover

In its statement, Corus said:

> "As we continue to adapt to the evolving needs of our business, Corus has made a small number of changes in select markets, including roles at Global BC, Global National, News 640 and talk radio. These changes are part of the difficult but necessary work to ensure our teams are structured in a sustainable way..."

The affected operations span both television and radio:

- **Global BC** — regional television news
- **Global National** — the network's flagship newscast
- **News 640 Toronto (CFIQ-AM)** — talk radio
- Additional talk radio roles

This follows a July 2026 round that affected roughly **45 unionized employees**, primarily at Global News in Alberta, and centralized Calgary and Edmonton newsroom functions to Toronto.

## Background: the financial picture behind the Corus Entertainment layoffs

Corus reported its fiscal 2026 third quarter results for the period ended **May 31, 2026**. The figures come from the company's own release.

| Measure | Q3 FY2026 | Change year over year |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidated revenue | **$249.4 million** | **−16%** |
| Television advertising revenue | **$120.3 million** | **−20%** |
| Television subscriber revenue | **$96.5 million** | **−13%** |
| Segment profit | **$29.1 million** | **−53%** |
| Net loss attributable to shareholders | **$36.5 million** | ($0.18 per share) |
| Free cash flow | **$6.2 million** | (−$46.1M over nine months) |
| Net debt to segment profit | **8.20x** | (6.01x at Aug 31, 2025) |

**John Gossling, Chief Executive Officer**, said the results "reflect significant savings from cost management initiatives, which helped partially offset continued pressure on linear television advertising demand."

Two lines matter most for anyone tracking the Canadian TV market.

**Advertising down 20%** is the demand side — advertisers following audiences to digital platforms.

**Subscriber revenue down 13%** is cord-cutting itself. Corus is paid per subscriber by cable, satellite and IPTV distributors that carry its specialty channels. When Canadian households drop or downsize a television package, that revenue falls whether or not anyone stops watching Global.

## Why the Corus Entertainment layoffs matter to viewers

The **Corus Entertainment layoffs** connect a household decision to an industry outcome that is rarely made explicit.

The conventional television bundle has cross-subsidized Canadian local news for decades. Specialty channel carriage fees and national advertising funded regional newsrooms. As the bundle shrinks, that funding shrinks with it — and newsrooms are among the most expensive things a broadcaster operates.

That is visible in how the cuts were structured. July's round did not just remove positions; it **moved Calgary and Edmonton newsroom functions to Toronto**. The service continues, produced from further away.

For cord-cutters, this is the genuine trade-off, and it is worth naming honestly. Streaming and IPTV replace entertainment programming comprehensively and cheaply. **Local news is the one category they replace least well** — there is no streaming equivalent of a regional supper-hour newscast with reporters in the community.

Corus is not an outlier. Rogers shed television subscribers through the first half of 2026, TELUS discontinued reporting TV subscriber numbers effective January 1, 2026, and CBC is closing its documentary Channel on August 31, 2026.

## What this does and does not tell us

A few limits are worth stating plainly, because coverage of broadcaster job cuts often overreaches.

**The company did not publish a number.** "A small number of changes" is not a headcount, and no verified total for 2026 exists. Any figure circulating without attribution to Corus or a union should be treated as unconfirmed.

**Falling subscriber revenue is not the same as falling viewership.** It measures carriage payments from distributors. Audiences can hold steady while the revenue attached to them shrinks, because the money follows the subscription, not the viewing.

**Corus has not announced channel closures alongside these cuts.** Programming decisions are separate from staffing decisions, and nothing in the August statement addressed carriage or channel lineups.

## Conclusion

The **Corus Entertainment layoffs** are small in scale and large in what they signal. A 13% decline in subscriber revenue is the aggregate of individual households deciding a television package is no longer worth the price — a decision that is usually rational for the household and structurally difficult for the broadcaster.

Corus reports its fiscal fourth quarter and full-year results later in 2026. Those filings, rather than any single announcement, will show whether the trend in carriage revenue is stabilizing.
