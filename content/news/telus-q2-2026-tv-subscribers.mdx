**TELUS Q2 2026** delivered a $1.8 billion net loss, a 55% dividend cut and a sharply lowered outlook — but for Canadians thinking about their TV package, the most telling detail was something that wasn't there. The quarter contained **no TV subscriber number at all**. TELUS discontinued video subscriber reporting under a methodology change effective January 1, 2026, and it is now the second major Canadian carrier in a year to change how it counts television customers.

*Published August 4, 2026. Figures are drawn from TELUS's published results for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, released July 31, 2026.*

![A viewer with a TV remote considering options after the TELUS Q2 2026 results](https://images.pexels.com/photos/5202959/pexels-photo-5202959.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## The TELUS Q2 2026 headline numbers

| Metric | TELUS Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Net loss | **$1.8 billion** | Driven by a $2.1B non-cash TELUS Digital impairment |
| Operating revenues | $4.9 billion | Service revenue down 1% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $1.8 billion | Down 2% |
| Annualized dividend | **$0.75** (from $1.6736) | **Cut 55%** |
| Net debt to adjusted EBITDA | 3.5x | Target ~3.0x by end-2028 |
| Internet net additions | 20,000 | — |
| Mobile phone net additions | 17,000 | — |
| Connected device net additions | 187,000 | — |
| **TV / video subscribers** | **Not reported** | Discontinued |

The dividend reset is the largest single decision in TELUS Q2 2026. Cutting the annualized payout from **$1.6736 to $0.75 per share** — a quarterly payment of $0.1875 — is expected to retain roughly **$2.7 billion in cash through 2028** for debt reduction. TELUS also terminated its dividend reinvestment plan discount effective **October 1, 2026**.

## 6 signals in TELUS Q2 2026 for Canadian TV viewers

### 1. The TV subscriber count is gone

Effective **January 1, 2026**, TELUS revised its reporting to focus on **mobility and internet**. Video subscriber data was discontinued, so TELUS Q2 2026 contains no Optik TV figure at all.

TELUS has not stated that this reflects declining TV customers, and no figure was published either way. What is verifiable is the change itself: a metric that existed is no longer disclosed.

### 2. TELUS is the second carrier to change how TV is counted

Bell made its own change effective the same date, **January 1, 2026**, redefining an IPTV subscriber to include **bundled streaming service subscribers** under a new "video subscriber" metric. To qualify, a customer must take a package including at least one third-party streaming service and one BCE streaming service.

Two of Canada's three national carriers redefining or retiring their TV metric in the same quarter is a pattern worth noticing — and it makes year-over-year comparison across the industry considerably harder for anyone outside the companies.

### 3. Rogers, which still reports, is losing subscribers quickly

The one large carrier still publishing a comparable figure **shed 111,000 TV subscribers in the first half of 2026**, as we reported in our coverage of [Rogers' Q2 results](/news/rogers-tv-subscriber-losses-q2-2026).

That provides the reference point the other two no longer supply.

### 4. Internet is growing while TV is not the story

TELUS added **20,000 internet** customers and **17,000 mobile phone** customers in the quarter, alongside **187,000 connected devices**.

This is the shape of the modern Canadian telecom: the broadband line is the product being defended, and television is increasingly something delivered over it rather than a separate business worth reporting on.

### 5. The TELUS Q2 2026 guidance cut was substantial

| Guidance | Previous | Revised |
|---|---|---|
| Service revenue | +2% to +4% | **Flat to −2%** |
| Adjusted EBITDA | +2% to +4% | **−2% to −4%** |
| Capital expenditure | ~$2.3 billion | **~$2.6 billion** |
| Free cash flow | ~$2.45 billion | **~$1.8 billion** |

Spending more while earning less, with leverage at 3.5x and a deleveraging target pushed out to **end-2028** from 2027, is a set of pressures that rarely produces cheaper consumer pricing.

### 6. The backdrop is a market that has already tipped

**48.5%** of Canadian households ended 2025 with no television subscription from a cable, satellite or telecom provider — up from 46% in 2024, and projected to reach **57% by 2028**. Traditional TV subscription revenue fell **5% to $6.2 billion**.

## Why TELUS Q2 2026 matters to your TV bill

For viewers, the practical consequence is not the loss itself — it is what a carrier under this kind of pressure tends to do next.

TELUS is now committed to spending **$2.6 billion** in capital while generating around **$1.8 billion** in free cash flow, servicing debt at 3.5x EBITDA, and having removed $2.7 billion of dividend commitments to fund the gap. Broadband is the asset it is defending. Television, in that framing, becomes a retention tool inside a bundle rather than a product sold on its own merits.

That has two implications for households reviewing their bills:

- **Bundle-dependent pricing is likely to persist.** When TV exists to protect an internet subscription, the attractive TV price usually requires the internet subscription.
- **Comparison is getting harder, not easier.** With TELUS no longer reporting video subscribers and Bell counting bundled streaming inside its video metric, the public numbers that once let consumers judge whether a provider was gaining or losing TV customers are disappearing.

## Key takeaways

- TELUS Q2 2026 showed a **$1.8 billion net loss** on **$4.9 billion** revenue, released **July 31, 2026**.
- The loss was driven largely by a **$2.1 billion non-cash impairment** of TELUS Digital.
- The dividend was **cut 55%**, from $1.6736 to **$0.75** annualized, retaining ~$2.7 billion through 2028.
- **No TV or video subscriber figure was reported** — the metric was discontinued effective January 1, 2026.
- **Bell** separately redefined its video subscriber metric effective the same date.
- **Rogers**, still reporting, lost **111,000 TV subscribers** in the first half of 2026.
- Guidance was cut across service revenue, EBITDA and free cash flow, while **capex rose to ~$2.6 billion**.

## The bottom line

Investors will remember TELUS Q2 2026 for the dividend cut. For Canadian TV viewers, the more consequential line is the one that disappeared: the country's second-largest carrier no longer tells the public how many television customers it has, and its largest rival has changed the definition.

The broader trend does not depend on those disclosures. Nearly half of Canadian households already have no traditional TV subscription, and that share is forecast to keep climbing.
