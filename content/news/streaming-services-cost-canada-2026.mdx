**Streaming services cost Canada 2026** more than most cord-cutters expect once you add everything up: ad-supported plans still start under $10 a month, but premium ad-free tiers now run $16 to $24 each — and stacking four or five of them quietly rivals a cable bill. This is a mid-year cost check of what the big mainstream services actually charge in Canada right now, why the "cheaper than cable" logic breaks when you stack, and how to keep the total down.

A few things stand out. The cheapest door into any single service is its ad tier — Paramount+ opens at $6.99, Netflix at $7.99. The jump to ad-free is real: an ad-supported plan costs an average of about 42% less than its ad-free equivalent, per the Couch Potato report. And Amazon is the outlier — Prime Video comes bundled with a $99-a-year Prime membership, then charges another $2.99 a month just to switch the ads off.

## Why "cheaper than cable" breaks when you stack

No single subscription looks expensive. The problem is that almost nobody keeps just one. Line up the premium, ad-free tiers of four big services — Netflix Premium ($23.99), Crave Premium ($22), Paramount+ Premium ($13.99) and Disney+ Premium ($16.99) — and you are already at about **$77 a month, or roughly $920 a year**, before adding a fifth app or an ad-free Prime upgrade. That is squarely in traditional cable-bill territory, which is the honest catch behind streaming services cost Canada 2026: the value case was built on picking one or two services, not collecting five.

Two forces make it worse each year. First, prices keep climbing — the ten leading providers raised prices an average of **7% in 2025**, with steeper hikes expected in 2026, a trend we track in [why streaming prices keep rising in Canada](/news/streaming-prices-canada-2026). Second, new costs arrive through the side door, from ad-free upcharges to fresh government levies. Individually each bump is easy to shrug off; together they are how a "cord-cutting" household rebuilds a cable-sized bill one app at a time.

## How to keep your streaming bill down

The fix is not to give up what you watch — it is to stop paying for what you do not.

- **Rotate, don't stack.** Subscribe to one or two services at a time, watch what you came for, then cancel and move to the next. Most catalogues are still there when you return.
- **Default to ad tiers.** If you only half-watch a service, the ad-supported plan — averaging about 42% cheaper — usually beats paying a premium to remove ads you barely notice.
- **Kill dormant add-ons.** Ad-free Prime, extra channels and premium upgrades are the easiest cuts; drop the ones you cannot remember using.
- **Add up your real total, then compare.** Put your all-in monthly number beside one flat-fee alternative. ## The bottom line

Streaming services cost Canada 2026 is no longer a simple "cheaper than cable" story. Ad-supported plans remain genuinely cheap, and one or two well-chosen subscriptions still beat a full cable package. But premium ad-free tiers now cost $16 to $24 each, prices are still rising, and stacking four or five of them lands you right back where cord-cutting started.
