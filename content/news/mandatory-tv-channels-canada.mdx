**Published August 19, 2026**

## Brief summary

On **September 1, 2026**, the wholesale rate for one of the **mandatory TV channels Canada** distributors are required to carry rises from **13 cents to 16 cents** per subscriber per month. The increase, for the Cable Public Affairs Channel, was approved in **Broadcasting Decision CRTC 2026-66** and depended on the Commission renewing CPAC's licence before the deadline — which it has now done, through **August 31, 2027**.

![mandatory TV channels Canada — a household reviewing television and internet service terms as regulated wholesale rates change](https://images.pexels.com/photos/12955517/pexels-photo-12955517.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Introduction

Three cents is not a story. The mechanism behind it is.

Most Canadians with a cable, satellite or telco TV subscription are paying, every month, for the mandatory TV channels Canada regulators require their provider to carry. The rates are set by the regulator, not negotiated. Almost nobody knows the list exists, and it is one of the few parts of a TV bill that changes by decision rather than by market.

## Main story: the mandatory TV channels Canada fee that changes

CPAC — the Cable Public Affairs Channel, which broadcasts Parliament's proceedings in English and French — applied to raise its mandatory per-subscriber wholesale rate.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Previous rate | **$0.13** per subscriber per month |
| New rate | **$0.16** per subscriber per month |
| Increase | **3 cents** |
| Effective | **September 1, 2026** |
| Decision | **Broadcasting Decision CRTC 2026-66** |
| Condition | Licence and mandatory distribution order renewed before that date |

The condition has since been satisfied. The CRTC has administratively renewed broadcasting licences for a range of television services, CPAC among them, running **September 1, 2026 to August 31, 2027**, as reported by Cartt.ca.

In approving the application, the Commission said CPAC's financial situation **"has continued to deteriorate and is now critical, jeopardizing the fulfillment of its mandate and the sustainability of its public interest mission."** CPAC had argued the increase was **"essential for CPAC to continue to provide its foundational public service to Canadians."**

### The decision was not unanimous

Commissioners **Ellen Desmond** and **Stéphanie Paquette** dissented, taking the position that relief should wait until a new regulatory framework is settled. **Bell, Quebecor Media and TELUS** had argued during the proceeding that distributors cannot absorb additional financial burdens in the current market.

That disagreement is the substance here. It is a dispute about who funds public-interest programming when the subscriber base paying for it is shrinking.

## Background: how mandatory TV channels Canada rules work

The CRTC can order that a service be distributed by every licensed television provider, using **paragraph 9.1(1)(h) of the Broadcasting Act**. A service granted that status also gets a set wholesale rate, paid per subscriber per month by each distributor.

CPAC has held mandatory distribution since **2002**.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who must carry the channel? | Every **licensed** cable, satellite and telco TV provider |
| Who sets the rate? | The **CRTC**, by decision — not commercial negotiation |
| Who pays it? | The **distributor**, per subscriber per month |
| Does it appear on your bill? | **No** — it is a wholesale cost, not an itemised charge |
| Do online streaming services pay it? | **No** — must-carry does not apply to them |

The sequence that led here:

1. **2002** — CPAC first granted a mandatory distribution order
2. **Broadcasting Decision 2023-245** — licences and mandatory distribution orders administratively renewed to **August 31, 2026**
3. **2024** — CPAC applies to raise its wholesale rate from 13 to 16 cents
4. **Broadcasting Decision 2025-312** — the Commission **defers** the application while examining broader structural issues in the industry
5. **Broadcasting Decision CRTC 2026-66** — increase approved, effective September 1, 2026, conditional on renewal
6. **August 2026** — licences administratively renewed to **August 31, 2027**; the condition is met

## Why mandatory TV channels Canada rules matter to subscribers

The direct financial effect on any individual subscriber is negligible. What makes the mandatory TV channels Canada framework worth knowing is the structure it reveals.

**A wholesale rate is not a line on your bill.** It is what your provider pays the channel. Whether a distributor passes on three cents, absorbs it, or rolls it into an unrelated price change is a commercial decision. If your package price does change, that is a separate event requiring its own notice under the CRTC's television code — which obliges at least 30 days' notice of a price increase.

**The obligation stops at licensed distributors.** Mandatory distribution binds cable, satellite and telco TV providers. Online-delivered services are not subject to must-carry, so anyone who has cancelled traditional TV contributes nothing toward these rates. That gap is one of the clearer practical differences between a regulated TV subscription and an internet-delivered one.

**The funding model is under strain.** The CRTC's own language — a financial situation now "critical" — describes a channel funded by a per-subscriber levy at a time when the number of subscribers is falling. Every service funded this way faces the same arithmetic: a fixed cost base divided by a shrinking denominator. The choice is a higher rate per remaining subscriber, or less service.

## Key takeaways

- CPAC's wholesale rate rises **13¢ → 16¢** per subscriber per month on **September 1, 2026**
- Approved in **Broadcasting Decision CRTC 2026-66**, conditional on licence renewal
- Licences administratively renewed **September 1, 2026 – August 31, 2027**, meeting the condition
- The CRTC called CPAC's financial position **"critical"**
- **Two commissioners dissented**; Bell, Quebecor and TELUS opposed
- The rate is paid by distributors — it is **not an itemised subscriber charge**
- Mandatory TV channels Canada rules bind **licensed cable, satellite and telco TV only**, not online services

## Conclusion

Among the **mandatory TV channels Canada** requires distributors to carry, CPAC is the one whose funding has now been formally described by the regulator as critical. A three-cent increase resolves that for a year.

The larger question the dissenting commissioners raised is unresolved: whether a per-subscriber levy is still a workable way to fund public-interest television when the subscriber base is contracting every quarter. The CRTC has a broader consultation running on consumer protections across television and telecom, and the funding structure sits alongside it. For now, one small number moves on September 1 — and most of the people paying it will never see it.
