Canadians will be able to **watch curling in Canada** across more channels, and more of it free, than at any point in the sport's broadcast history. The Grand Slam of Curling and Rock League announced five-year agreements with Sportsnet and CBC on **August 13, 2026**, delivering more than **300 hours** of linear television in the 2026-27 season — roughly double recent years.

![Watch curling in Canada — comparing live sport broadcast options across television and streaming services](https://images.pexels.com/photos/12788370/pexels-photo-12788370.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Summary

- **Five-year** agreements with **Sportsnet** and **CBC**
- **300+ hours** of linear TV in 2026-27, about **double** previous coverage
- Grand Slam of Curling starts **October 2026**; Rock League returns **January 2027**
- Free options: **CBC television**, **CBC Gem**, **ICI TOU.TV**
- Paid options: **Sportsnet**, **Sportsnet+**

## Where to watch curling in Canada, event by event

This is the practical part of how to watch curling in Canada this season. The rights are split by day of the week, which is unusual and worth understanding before the season starts.

| Coverage | Platform | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Grand Slam: 3 draws daily, Tue–Fri, all 5 events | Sportsnet | Paid |
| Grand Slam: Saturday playoffs | CBC, **CBC Gem**, Sportsnet+ | **Free** (CBC/Gem) |
| Grand Slam: Sunday championship finals | Sportsnet | Paid |
| Grand Slam: morning round robin + tiebreakers, Tue–Sat | Rock Channel | Live stream |
| Rock League: every game (from Jan 2027) | CBC TV, **CBC Gem** | **Free** |
| Rock League: select games | ICI TOU.TV | **Free** |
| Rock League: select Thursday/Friday games | Sportsnet | Paid |

CBC also carries select finals through the season.

For anyone who wants to watch curling in Canada without a sports subscription, the Saturday playoff windows and the entire Rock League schedule on CBC Gem are the key entries.

## What the parties said

**Nic Sulsky**, CEO and co-founder of The Curling Group, called it "a defining moment for the sport," adding that "Sportsnet and CBC Sports see the opportunity to build on curling's incredible heritage and momentum."

**Greg Sansone**, president of Sportsnet, said the broadcaster is "expanding our coverage to give fans more curling on Sportsnet than ever before."

**Chris Wilson**, executive director of sports and Olympics at CBC, said "CBC has a long history of curling coverage, and by providing a national platform to these premier curling tournaments for another five years, we hope to engage and entertain an even wider audience."

## Background: a notable contrast with hockey

The announcement lands in a season defined by the opposite trend in Canada's biggest sport.

CBC's national NHL sublicence was not renewed after the 2025-26 playoffs, ending decades of free over-the-air national hockey. From **September 30, 2026**, Rogers' new 12-year sublicence gives Amazon Prime Video at least 26 national Wednesday games a year plus selected playoff series, while Sportsnet retains 500-plus national games. Sportsnet+ pricing rises to **$34.99** monthly for Standard and **$44.99** for Premium from **September 22, 2026**.

Rogers also confirmed on **August 14, 2026** that *Hockey Night in Canada: Punjabi* would not return after 18 years, saying that "while audience levels aren't strong enough to sustain the production long term, we know these broadcasts have meant a lot to many Punjabi-speaking hockey fans."

Against that backdrop, a five-year expansion of curling coverage — with a substantial free component — runs the other way.

## Why this matters

**The ability to watch curling in Canada for free is expanding while hockey moves the other way.** Households that lost free national hockey are gaining a materially larger volume of free national curling. For viewers who rely on over-the-air television or free streaming, that is a real change in what is available at no cost.

**It suits cord-cutters structurally.** CBC Gem and ICI TOU.TV are free streaming services requiring no cable subscription, so the free portion of this deal is fully accessible to anyone with an internet connection.

**The split-rights model requires planning.** Because coverage moves between Sportsnet, CBC, CBC Gem, Sportsnet+ and the Rock Channel depending on the day and round, following a single event end-to-end may mean switching platforms mid-week. Anyone budgeting subscriptions should map which rounds they actually care about before paying for a sports tier.

**Live streams run behind the broadcast.** As with any live sport delivered over the internet, expect a delay of several seconds to half a minute against the live event — worth remembering if you follow scores elsewhere.

## Watch curling in Canada: key takeaways

1. **Five-year deals** with Sportsnet and CBC, announced **August 13, 2026**.
2. **300+ hours** of coverage in 2026-27 — about **double** previous seasons.
3. **Saturday Grand Slam playoffs and all Rock League games are free** on CBC and CBC Gem.
4. **Grand Slam begins October 2026**; **Rock League returns January 2027**.
5. **Rights are split by day**, so plan which platform you need for which round.

## Conclusion

The commercial story behind the new deals is a rights renewal. The consumer story is that a significant block of live Canadian sport is moving toward free distribution at the same time as hockey moves further behind paywalls.

For cord-cutters, that makes curling one of the better-value live sports on the Canadian schedule this season — most of the marquee weekend rounds cost nothing beyond an internet connection.
