## Four premieres, four platforms: the stacking trap

Nothing here is a trap by design — it is a coincidence of scheduling. But the effect on a household budget is real. Add one subscription per premiere and you have four active bills by August 1, each renewing on a different date, most of them for a single show you will finish in a few weeks.

This is the same squeeze we broke down in our look at [streaming prices climbing again in Canada](/news/streaming-prices-canada-2026): individual services still look affordable, and the total is what hurts. The premiere calendar simply compresses months of that decision-making into nine days.

## The rotation play

The fix is boring and it works: **stop subscribing in parallel and start subscribing in sequence.**

- **Check what you already have first.** If you hold an Amazon Prime membership at $99 a year, Prime Video is already included — Batman: Caped Crusader costs you nothing additional.
- **Rotate by premiere.** Take Disney+ for the month The Proud Family finishes its run, then drop it and move to whatever premieres next. Seasons release over weeks; your subscription does not have to overlap them all.
- **Use ad tiers when you are there for one show.** Disney+ starts at $8.99 a month with ads and Paramount+ at $6.99 a month. Watching a single series through an ad tier is the cheapest legitimate way to follow it.
- **Treat promos as temporary.** Peacock's promotional $3.99 a month is genuinely cheap — and it reverts to $10.99. The promo is only a saving if you diarize the revert date.

Three habits do most of the work here.
