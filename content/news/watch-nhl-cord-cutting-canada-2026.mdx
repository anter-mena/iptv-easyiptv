If you want to **watch NHL without cable** in Canada this season, the rules just changed under your feet. As of June 2026, CBC's long-standing arrangement to air *Hockey Night in Canada* for free has expired, which means there's no longer a free national broadcast of NHL games. Rogers-owned Sportsnet is now the only televised home of the league in Canada — and its streaming price has climbed sharply. Here's an honest breakdown of what happened, what your real options are now, and how to keep watching hockey without your monthly bill quietly ballooning.

## What Changed: CBC's Free Hockey Night in Canada Is Over

For years, one of the quiet comforts of Canadian hockey was that you could catch a national game on CBC without paying a cent. That era is done. CBC's deal to simulcast *Hockey Night in Canada* ran through the end of the 2025-26 season and was **not renewed** — so from June 2026 onward, CBC no longer carries free national NHL games.

The practical effect is simple but significant: the one no-cost route to live NHL on your TV has closed. Anyone who leaned on that free broadcast — cord-cutters, antenna users, or households that simply never paid for sports — now has to sign up for something. And "something," in 2026, means Rogers.

![Two hockey fans watching an NHL game on a living-room TV, the new reality for anyone trying to watch NHL without cable in Canada](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6557551/pexels-photo-6557551.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Sportsnet Is Now the Only Game in Town — and It Costs More

With CBC out of the picture, **Sportsnet is the sole televised home of the NHL in Canada.** If you want the games, you need a Sportsnet subscription — either through a cable package or through the standalone streaming service, Sportsnet+.

And Sportsnet+ is not the bargain it once was. The plan that cost **$14.99/month** ($179/year) back in 2023 now runs **$29.99/month**, or **$249.90/year**. That's roughly a doubling in about three years, landing right as it became the only way to stream the league.

| Sportsnet+ streaming | Back in 2023 | Now (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly price | $14.99 | $29.99 |
| Annual price | $179 | $249.90 |
| Free national NHL on CBC | Available | Gone |

The timing isn't a coincidence. When a service becomes the *only* option, there's little pressure to keep it cheap. For hockey fans, that's the sting: less choice and a higher price arriving in the same season.

### The bigger problem: sports rights are scattered

Even paying for Sportsnet+ doesn't get you *everything*. Canadian sports rights are fragmented across platforms — different leagues, and even different games, live on different services, and no single subscription carries the whole slate. Follow more than one sport and you can quickly end up with two or three overlapping paid apps just to see the events you care about.

That fragmentation is the real modern headache. It's the same "streaming stack" trap we covered in our look at [streaming price increases in 2026](/news/streaming-cable-price-increases-2026): the more the content splinters, the more separate bills you accumulate to reassemble it.

## How to Watch NHL Without Cable in 2026

So what are your actual options now that the free broadcast is gone? There are three honest routes to **watch NHL without cable** this season:

1. **Sportsnet+ streaming.** The direct path — $29.99/month gets you the league over the internet, no cable box required. It's the simplest single-sport answer, if hockey is all you want.
2. **A Sportsnet cable/TV package.** Still an option through your provider, but you're back to a set-top box, contracts, and the surcharges that pushed people to cut the cord in the first place.
3. **"Churn and rotate."** The strategy pundits are pushing for 2026: subscribe to a service for the part of the season or the events you care about, then cancel and move to the next one. It saves money but takes real effort and calendar-watching.

None of these is wrong. But notice the pattern — each one either costs more than it used to, locks you back into hardware you left behind, or turns watching your team into a subscription-management chore.

## Why Live Sports Keeps Cable Expensive

Here's the part worth stepping back for. Live sports — and the NHL especially — has always been the anchor keeping people on cable. You can stream a drama or a comedy anywhere, but live games have historically been locked behind the pricey bundle, and that single fact is why millions kept paying for TV packages they otherwise didn't want.

The 2026 twist is that hockey is now **paywalled, pricier, and scattered** all at once. The free door closed, the streaming price doubled, and the games are split across services. For fans, it's the worst of every trend converging in one season.

Meanwhile, cord-cutting keeps accelerating. Canadian traditional-TV households have fallen by more than **1.2 million over five years**, with pay-TV penetration sliding from about **75% in 2015 to roughly 51% in 2021**, and hovering near **49% since**. People are leaving cable — but sports is the thread pulling some of them back, and providers know it.

## What a Hockey Season Actually Costs Now

It's easy to underestimate the real number, because the price you see is monthly but the season is long. Play it out. If you take Sportsnet+ at $29.99/month and keep it through the full October-to-June regular season and playoffs — roughly nine months — you're at about **$270 for hockey alone**, before a single other subscription. Add one general-entertainment service for the household at around $20 a month and you've cleared **$450 for the year** without touching a second sport.

The "churn and rotate" trick trims that, but not painlessly. To do it well you have to track which service carries which games, set cancellation reminders so you aren't billed straight through the summer, and accept that you'll miss the odd matchup that lands on a platform you just dropped. For a lot of households, the few dollars saved aren't worth turning game night into a spreadsheet.

And none of that counts the hardware side. A cable package drags a set-top box and its rental fee back into your living room; the streaming route at least runs on gear you already own. That's the quiet advantage of app-based viewing — the device in your hand or under your TV is the whole setup, with nothing to bolt to the wall and no technician visit to book. When you tally the season pass, the rental fees, and the second and third apps you signed up for along the way, the gap between the old cable math and a single streaming bill only gets wider.
