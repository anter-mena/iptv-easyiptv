With the final just days away, the biggest question for soccer fans north of the border is simply **how to watch World Cup 2026 in Canada** without missing a kick. The 2026 FIFA World Cup — the first-ever 48-team tournament, co-hosted by Canada, the United States and Mexico — reaches its climax this week, and the whole thing comes down to one match at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey on Sunday, July 19. If you cut the cord years ago, or you simply aren't sure which channel or app has the game, this is the plain-English guide to catching the final — and the semifinals before it — on whatever screen you already own.

## Quick Answer

Short on time? Here's the bottom line. In English, the 2026 World Cup is carried by **Bell Media**, which means the games air on **TSN** (channels TSN1, TSN3, TSN4 and TSN5) and on **CTV**. CTV is showing every Canada match plus the marquee knockout rounds — including both semifinals, the third-place playoff and the **final on Sunday, July 19 at 3 p.m. ET**. In French, the matches run on **RDS**, with Canada's games and the final also airing free-to-air on **Noovo**. To stream without cable, **TSN+** carries all 104 matches for around **$29.99 a month**. The two semifinals were played Tuesday and Wednesday, July 14 and 15, so we're right at the sharp end of the tournament.

## Who Broadcasts the World Cup in Canada

There's only one name to remember: **Bell Media**. It holds the exclusive Canadian rights to the 2026 FIFA World Cup and is carrying all 104 matches across its family of networks, in both official languages. That single fact makes the whole "where's the game?" puzzle a lot simpler than it looks.

**In English**, coverage is split between two of Bell's channels. The bulk of the tournament lives on **TSN** — spread across TSN1, TSN3, TSN4 and TSN5 so that overlapping kickoffs each get their own channel — while **CTV**, the free over-the-air network, takes the crown-jewel fixtures. CTV's slate includes every Canada match, a handful of knockout-round games, **both semifinals, the third-place match and the final itself**. Because CTV broadcasts free to air, that means the July 19 final is reachable with nothing more than a basic antenna.

**In French**, the home of the tournament is **RDS** (with overflow on RDS2), which is running full French-language commentary and studio coverage on all 104 games. On top of that, **Noovo** — a free-to-air French network — is carrying Canada's fixtures and the final, so francophone fans have a no-cost route to the biggest matches too.

For the record, the semifinals were staged on July 14 and 15, the third-place playoff on July 18, and the final caps everything on Sunday, July 19, kicking off at 3 p.m. Eastern from MetLife Stadium — officially rebranded "New York New Jersey Stadium" for the tournament — in East Rutherford, New Jersey.

## How to Watch World Cup 2026 in Canada Without Cable

Here's the good news for cord-cutters: you don't need a cable box for any of this. If you're weighing up **how to watch World Cup 2026 in Canada** using only the internet, you've got several honest options, and none of them require a technician visit.

The most complete streaming route is **TSN+**, Bell Media's standalone service. It streams all 104 matches — group stage through final — for about **$29.99 a month**, no TV package required. If you already pay for TSN through a provider, you can sign into **TSN.ca or the TSN app** and stream the same games at no extra cost with your login. For the free CTV games, the **CTV app and CTV.ca** stream those broadcasts, and some fixtures also surface on **Crave**. TSN coverage is even available as an add-on through Amazon Prime Video, if that's where you already watch.

### Free versus paid: what you actually pay for

It helps to separate the two. The **free** tier is genuinely useful for the big games: CTV airs the final and semifinals over the air, Noovo does the same in French, and TSN's official YouTube channel streams every pre-game show plus the first ten minutes of each match for nothing. That's plenty if you only care about the marquee fixtures.

The **paid** tier is what you need for *everything* — every group game, every round-of-32 tie, every quarterfinal. That's where a TSN or TSN+ subscription (or an all-in-one service that already includes those channels) earns its keep. The choice comes down to a simple question: do you want just the final, or the whole tournament and the live sports that follow it all year?
