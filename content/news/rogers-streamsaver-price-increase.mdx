A **Rogers StreamSaver price increase** takes the Netflix, Disney+ and Apple TV bundle from **$22 to $24 per month on August 18, 2026**, according to Rogers' own StreamSaver pages. On its own, a $2 monthly change is minor. What makes it worth flagging is the date: **August 18 is also when Rogers' separate $7 monthly home internet increase takes effect**, meaning a household with both would see roughly $9 a month in increases arrive on a single billing cycle.

## What the Rogers StreamSaver Price Increase Covers

Rogers Xfinity StreamSaver bundles three streaming services into one line on a Rogers bill. The included tiers are the **ad-supported** versions of Netflix and Disney+, together with Apple TV — a detail worth confirming, because the bundle is sometimes compared against ad-free pricing, which is not what it contains. Rogers has also offered the ability to add its Sportsnet+ service to the package at a discount.

When the bundle launched, Rogers positioned $22 as a saving of more than 30% against the individual list prices of the three services, which it cited as totalling $31.97. That comparison was accurate at launch. Whether the same gap holds in August 2026 depends on where each service's standalone pricing has moved in the interim — which is why the only reliable test is to price the exact tiers you would otherwise buy, today, and set that against $24.

## Why the Date Matters More Than the Amount

The Rogers StreamSaver price increase is small enough that most households would absorb it without much thought. The reason to pay attention is that it does not arrive alone.

Rogers is separately raising the monthly fee on certain home internet packages by **$7 plus taxes**, effective on the first bill issued on or after **August 18, 2026** — the change we covered in our report on the [Rogers internet price increase](/news/rogers-internet-price-increase). A customer holding both an affected internet plan and StreamSaver is therefore looking at approximately **$9 more per month**, or **$108 over a year**, appearing simultaneously and on the same statement.

That combination is easy to misread as a single large increase or, more often, to miss entirely because it lands across two separate line items.

## Background: The Cord-Cutter's Squeeze

The Rogers StreamSaver price increase fits a broader pattern that streaming households have been living with for two years. Dropping a traditional television package produces a real saving. But the two things that replace it — a home internet connection and a set of streaming subscriptions — have both been rising.

The internet connection is the harder of the two to avoid, because every streaming household needs one regardless of which services it subscribes to. When that line rises, it erodes the saving from cutting cable without offering an obvious substitute. Bundles like StreamSaver were built partly as a response to that squeeze, packaging services at a discount to slow subscription fatigue. A bundle raising its own price is a signal that the underlying content costs are still climbing.

## Why This Matters — and Your Smart Options

A $2 increase does not warrant panic. The Rogers StreamSaver price increase does warrant one honest audit, though, because most households are paying for more streaming than they watch.

**1. Price the bundle against its parts, today.** Add the current list prices of Netflix Standard with ads, Disney+ Standard with ads and Apple TV. If the total sits meaningfully above $24, the bundle still works. If it does not, you are paying for convenience rather than savings.

**2. Check what you actually watch.** Two weeks of honest logging usually reveals that one of the three services carries almost all the household's viewing — and that is a decision you can act on.

**3. Use the new fee rules on the internet side.** This is the most valuable option available and the least known. Under **Telecom Regulatory Policy CRTC 2026-43**, in force since **June 12, 2026**, providers can no longer charge activation fees, plan modification fees, or early cancellation fees on internet and wireless plans where no device is being financed. Changing or downgrading your internet plan is now free of those charges — a real shift in negotiating position. Note that television service falls under a separate code and is not covered.

**4. Ask about retention pricing.** Providers frequently apply a discount rather than lose a subscriber. Call before the change takes effect, not after.

**5. Reassess the whole stack.** If your household is still paying for a legacy TV package alongside streaming, our guide to [switching from cable to IPTV](/blog/switch-from-cable-to-iptv-canada) walks through the order of operations that protects your internet pricing while you make the change.
