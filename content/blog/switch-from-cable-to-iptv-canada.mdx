Households that **switch from cable to IPTV Canada** wide usually lose money on the first bill, and almost always for the same reason: they cancelled in the wrong order. As of June 12, 2026 the CRTC's fee ban made it free to change or cancel an internet plan — but that decision amended the Internet Code and the Wireless Code, not the television rules. So the service you are keeping became flexible while the service you are leaving stayed exactly as it was. Handle them in the wrong sequence and you can pay an early cancellation charge on the TV side while forfeiting a bundle discount on the internet side, in the same week. This is the nine-step sequence that avoids both.

## Quick Answer: Why You Switch From Cable to IPTV Canada in This Order

Two consumer codes now govern the two halves of this decision, and since June 2026 they say different things.

| | Internet and wireless | Television |
|---|---|---|
| Governing code | Internet Code / Wireless Code | Television Service Provider Code |
| Activation fees | Banned since June 12, 2026 | Not covered by that decision |
| Plan modification fees | Banned since June 12, 2026 | Not covered by that decision |
| Early cancellation charge | Banned where no device is financed | Can still apply inside a fixed term |
| Notice to cancel | No 30-day notice requirement | No 30-day notice requirement |
| Notice of price increases | Per code | At least 30 calendar days |

The practical instruction falls straight out of that table: **change your internet arrangement first, while doing so is free, and cancel television last.** People who do it the other way round give up their negotiating position — once the TV service is gone, so is the bundle, and so is the leverage.

## Step 1: Watch Yourself for Two Weeks Before You Decide Anything

Before comparing anything, keep a two-week log of what the household actually watches — channel, day, and who was watching. It is the least glamorous part of any plan to switch from cable to IPTV Canada households consider, and it prevents the most expensive mistake. Almost everyone discovers the same two things: the real list is far shorter than the package, and one or two items on it are non-negotiable for a specific person.

That list is the spec sheet for everything that follows. A household whose must-haves are national news and general entertainment has a very easy switch. A household built around one exclusive sports package has a harder problem that is better identified now than in October.

## Step 2: Read Your TV Contract for the Early Cancellation Formula

This is the number that sets your timing when you switch from cable to IPTV Canada providers can turn on within a day. Your written television agreement must set out how an early cancellation charge is calculated, and the key figures should also appear in the Critical Information Summary your provider gave you at signing. Find three numbers:

- Your commitment end date, if you have one.
- The early cancellation formula and what it produces today.
- Any equipment still assigned to the account.

If you are out of contract, there is nothing to pay and nothing to time. If you are inside one, you now have a real number to weigh — and sometimes the arithmetic says wait two months rather than pay it.

## Step 3: Reprice Internet Before You Switch From Cable to IPTV Canada

This is the step that pays for the whole exercise, and it is only free because of a rule change most people have not registered.

Telecom Regulatory Policy CRTC 2026-43 amended the Internet Code and the Wireless Code to ban activation fees, plan modification fees, and early cancellation fees where no device is being financed, effective June 12, 2026. Changing an internet plan — up, down, or sideways — no longer carries a modification charge for individual and small business customers.

So before you touch the television service, get one thing in writing: **the exact monthly price of the internet plan you intend to keep, on its own, with the TV service removed.** Not the current bundled line item. Not "roughly the same." The standalone number, including any promotional expiry date.

Two realities to price in while you are on that call. Bundle discounts are usually conditional on the bundle existing, so removing television can reprice everything else. And standalone internet is not a static cost either — Rogers raised many internet prices by $7 per month from August 18, 2026, which is a reminder to ask when the quoted price expires as well as what it is.

## The Cost Worksheet: What You Are Actually Comparing

Most people compare the wrong two numbers — the TV line item against the IPTV subscription price. That comparison flatters the switch and hides the bundle effect. Build the worksheet with six rows instead, and fill in the right-hand column only with figures you have been quoted in writing.

| Line | Today | After the switch |
|---|---|---|
| Internet | Bundled price | Standalone price, with expiry date |
| Television | Bundled price | IPTV subscription |
| Equipment rental | Box and remote fees | Usually none; one-time device cost |
| Add-ons | Sports tiers, extra boxes | Any separate sports subscription |
| One-time costs | — | Early cancellation charge, antenna, device |
| Promotional expiry | When today's discount ends | When the new price ends |

Two rows decide most outcomes. The internet row is where a bundle discount disappears, and the one-time costs row is where an early cancellation charge lands. A household that ignores both can convince itself it is saving $60 a month and discover it is saving $18.

Run the worksheet over twelve months rather than one. A switch that looks marginal in month one often looks decisive across a year, because the one-time costs stop repeating while the monthly gap does not.

## Step 4: Confirm the Connection Can Carry Live TV

Live IPTV is less forgiving than on-demand streaming because there is very little pre-buffered content to absorb a hiccup. Jitter and packet loss matter more than headline speed.

Before you cancel anything, run the main television on the new service over an Ethernet cable during your household's busiest evening hour. If it holds up on a weeknight at 8pm with everyone home, it will hold up. Our [internet speed for IPTV](/blog/internet-speed-for-iptv) guide covers the specific thresholds; the short version is that wiring the primary screen resolves the large majority of buffering complaints on its own.

## Step 5: Run Both Services in Parallel for One Cycle

Overlap for two to four weeks. Yes, it means one duplicated bill, and it is the cheapest insurance available to anyone about to switch from cable to IPTV Canada providers deliver over their existing broadband. It also means the household validates the new setup against real viewing — a live sports night, a kids' morning, a big season premiere — while the old service is still there as a fallback.

Every switch that goes badly has the same root cause: cancelling first and discovering the gap afterwards, with no way back except a new install and possibly a new contract. One month of overlap is cheap insurance against that.

## Step 6: Close the Three Predictable Gaps

Nearly every household that moves off cable hits the same three gaps, and all three have straightforward answers if you plan them before you cancel rather than after.

- **Local and over-the-air channels.** Regional news is the single most-missed item. A basic indoor antenna restores free over-the-air broadcasts in most urban areas at a one-time cost.
- **Exclusive live sports.** Some rights are national and exclusive, so a specific league or team may need its own subscription. Identify which ones from your step 1 log rather than assuming.
- **Emergency alerting.** Alerts reach broadcast distribution services differently than they reach an internet video app. Keep wireless public alerting enabled on phones, and an antenna or radio in the house.

## Step 7: Cancel the Television Service Properly

Only now, with everything else in place, do you make the call that completes the switch from cable to IPTV Canada rules make easier than most people expect. You do not owe 30 days' notice. The CRTC prohibits television service providers from requiring 30 days' notice before cancellation, so treat any such demand as a mistake to be escalated rather than a rule to be obeyed.

When you call:

- State a specific cancellation date.
- Ask for a **cancellation reference number** and the agent's name.
- Ask for the final bill amount, whether the last period is pro-rated, and the exact early cancellation charge if one applies.
- Ask for written confirmation by email before you hang up.

Expect a retention offer. Whether to take it is a judgement call, but decide against the standalone internet number you obtained in step 3 — a retention discount that expires in six months is not the same as a permanent saving.

## Step 8: Return the Equipment and Keep the Receipt

Set-top boxes, remotes, and modems assigned to the television service usually have to come back, and unreturned-equipment charges are one of the most common billing disputes in this category.

Return everything in one trip, in its original packaging where you still have it, and get an itemised receipt listing serial numbers. Photograph the receipt. If you ship rather than drop off, keep the tracking number until the charge has cleared your account.

## Step 9: Audit the Next Two Bills

The saving from a decision to switch from cable to IPTV Canada pricing now makes attractive is real only if the bills confirm it. Check the first two statements after the change for four things: the early cancellation charge matches the contract formula, the internet line matches the standalone quote from step 3, no equipment charge has appeared, and any pro-rated amounts make arithmetic sense.

If something is wrong and the provider will not resolve it, the Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services administers the Television Service Provider Code and handles complaints against participating providers. That escalation route exists precisely for this kind of dispute.

## Is Now the Time to Switch From Cable to IPTV Canada?

You are not moving against the current. Rogers shed roughly 111,000 television subscribers in the first half of 2026, and the reporting picture itself has thinned — TELUS stopped publishing TV and video subscriber counts effective January 1, 2026, and Bell redefined its video metric on the same date. When incumbents stop publishing a number, it is rarely because the number is improving.

None of that means every household should switch. It does mean the pricing power that used to sit entirely with the incumbent has moved, and that a household choosing to switch from cable to IPTV Canada providers now serve over ordinary broadband is doing something increasingly ordinary rather than something exotic.

One caveat worth stating plainly. A Federal Court order issued on July 3, 2026 requires Canadian internet providers to block a list of piracy platforms for two years, with a faster process for adding new domains. That order targets unlicensed operations, not IPTV as a technology — but it does mean a provider's licensing posture is now a service-continuity question as well as a legal one. Choose a provider that is straightforward about what it carries and why.

## Actionable Takeaways

- Do it in order: reprice the internet first while modification fees are banned, cancel television last.
- Get the standalone internet price in writing, with its expiry date, before anything else changes.
- Find the early cancellation formula in your TV agreement and the Critical Information Summary — that number decides your timing.
- Overlap the two services for one cycle and test live IPTV over Ethernet at peak evening hours.
- You owe no 30-day notice. Get a cancellation reference, an itemised equipment receipt, and check the next two bills line by line.
