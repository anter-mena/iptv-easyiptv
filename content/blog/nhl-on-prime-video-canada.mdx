**NHL on Prime Video Canada** begins on 30 September 2026, and it is a more significant change than the game count suggests. A shopping membership is now the only route to a slice of the Canadian hockey schedule — including three playoff series a year. Here is exactly what is behind it, what it costs, and where the practical catches are.

![A smart TV home screen showing the app tiles behind NHL on Prime Video Canada coverage](https://images.pexels.com/photos/35490296/pexels-photo-35490296.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Quick Answer

Under a **twelve-year** sublicensing agreement between Rogers and Amazon, Prime Video holds **exclusive** Canadian rights to Wednesday night national NHL games from **30 September 2026**: at least **26 regular season games** a year, plus **two first-round and one second-round playoff series**, in **English and French**, **included with Prime** at no extra cost. Exclusive means exactly that — no simulcast anywhere else.

## NHL on Prime Video Canada: The Six Facts That Matter

**1. It is a twelve-year commitment, not a trial.** Rogers and Amazon announced the agreement as a long-term arrangement making Prime Video the home of Wednesday Night Hockey in Canada. This is not a one-season experiment that will revert.

**2. The volume is at least 26 national regular season games annually.** That is the floor rather than the ceiling, and they sit on Wednesday nights.

**3. The playoff rights are the underrated part.** Two first-round series and one second-round series each year. A first-round series can run seven games, so in a season where your team draws a Prime-carried series, a large share of your playoff viewing lives there.

**4. Both official languages.** Coverage is produced in English and French, which is a meaningful improvement on the patchwork that French-language viewers have often faced with streaming rights.

**5. There is no sports add-on.** The games are included with a standard Prime membership at $9.99 a month. Nothing further to purchase.

**6. Exclusive means no alternative.** These games are not on Sportsnet, not on TSN, not on cable. Without Prime there is no legal way to watch them in Canada.

## What "Included With Prime" Really Means

At $9.99 a month, Prime is the cheapest access point to any part of the NHL schedule in Canada — well below Sportsnet+ Standard at $34.99 from 22 September 2026, or TSN at $29.99.

Two consequences are worth drawing out.

**A lot of people already have it and do not know they have hockey.** Prime memberships bought for delivery, or shared within a household, now include a slice of the NHL season. If you are budgeting for the season, check whether the household already pays for Prime before adding anything.

**The reverse is also true.** Hockey is now a reason people will keep a Prime membership they might otherwise cancel — which is precisely why a retailer bid for sports rights in the first place. That is not a criticism, just worth naming: the hockey is included because the membership is the product.

## The Streaming-Only Catch for NHL on Prime Video Canada

Every other route to NHL hockey in Canada has a broadcast or cable feed behind it. Prime Video does not. That makes your home network a single point of failure on Wednesday nights, and it is worth preparing for.

Live sport is uniquely demanding, for a reason that surprises people: **the problem is consistency, not speed**. A live stream keeps only a few seconds of video buffered ahead of you. A two-second interruption therefore empties the buffer and produces a visible stutter, while a connection running at half the bandwidth but perfectly steady will look flawless. This is why a game can stall on a connection that streams films without complaint.

Practical steps, in order of impact:

1. **Run Ethernet to the main television.** The single highest-value change, and it removes Wi-Fi variability entirely.
2. **If Ethernet is impossible, use 5 GHz** and put the television and router in line of sight where you can.
3. **Expect evening congestion** roughly between 7 p.m. and 11 p.m., when neighbourhood demand peaks — which is exactly when games are on.
4. **Budget bandwidth for the household**, not the television. A game in HD wants a steady 25 Mbps or so; 4K wants nearer 40 to 50 Mbps, and that is on top of everything else in the house.
5. **Test with a full game** before 30 September. A ten-second check proves nothing; buffer problems show up at the twenty-minute mark.

## About the Delay

One thing to accept rather than fix: your stream runs **behind** the live event, commonly by five to thirty seconds. That is inherent to how streaming is delivered — video is cut into chunks, encoded at several quality levels, distributed and reassembled — and no setting on your television removes it.

The practical consequences are social rather than technical. A radio call, a phone alert, a group chat or a neighbour's shout through the wall will beat your screen to a goal. If that bothers you, mute notifications for the duration. It is not a fault, and it is not something a faster connection will solve.

## Getting the Picture Right for Hockey

Hockey is the hardest thing most televisions are asked to display. A puck moving at speed across a bright, uniform white surface, with fast horizontal pans and crowd detail in the background, exposes weaknesses that a film never will. A few settings make a visible difference.

**Turn motion smoothing off.** Marketed under a different name by every manufacturer, it interpolates frames and introduces lag — and on hockey it produces visible artefacts around the puck. Off is better.

**Use Game or Sport picture mode**, not Cinema. Cinema modes warm the image and soften motion, which is precisely wrong here.

**Do not force upscaling settings.** Let the television handle the incoming resolution rather than applying aggressive sharpening, which turns ice texture into noise.

**Check the frame rate the stream is delivering.** Sport benefits enormously from 50 or 60 frames per second, and some devices default to a lower output. If your device has a "match content frame rate" option, enable it.

**Sit closer than you think.** The detail advantage of a higher-resolution feed is only visible within a certain distance; beyond it you are paying for bitrate you cannot see.

## Is NHL on Prime Video Canada Worth It?

For $9.99 a month, if you already have Prime, the question does not really arise — it is free hockey attached to something you already buy.

If you do not have Prime, the honest calculation is narrower. Twenty-six regular season games spread across the whole league may include very few involving your team. The playoff series are the stronger argument, and they are unpredictable in advance. A reasonable strategy for a single-team fan is to skip Prime through the regular season and subscribe monthly in April if the draw puts your club in a Prime-carried series.

NHL on Prime Video Canada is a real, permanent part of the hockey map now — twelve years, 26-plus games a season, three playoff series a year, in both languages, at no extra cost above a $9.99 membership.
