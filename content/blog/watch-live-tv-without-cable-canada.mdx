If you want to **watch live TV without cable in Canada**, the good news is that in 2026 you have more ways to do it — and more ways to do it cheaply — than at any point before. A cable box is no longer the price of admission for live channels, sports, news, or your favourite shows. All you really need is an internet connection and a device you probably already own. This guide walks through every honest route to cutting the cord, what each one costs, what you keep, and how to replace the one thing cable-cutters always worry about: live sports.

## Quick Answer

You can **watch live TV without cable in Canada** through four main routes: a free over-the-air antenna for local channels, individual streaming apps, dedicated live-TV streaming services, or an all-in-one IPTV subscription that bundles live TV, sports, and on-demand into a single app. Most people mix an antenna for local stations with one paid service for everything else — or skip the juggling entirely and use one all-in-one service. The only real requirements are a stable internet connection (around 10 Mbps per HD stream) and a device such as a Fire TV Stick, an Android TV box, or a smart TV.

## Why So Many Canadians Are Cutting Cable

The move away from cable isn't a fad — it's a steady, years-long shift. Traditional TV subscriptions in Canada have been sliding for a decade as households look at their bill and ask what they're actually paying for. A typical cable package bundles dozens of channels you never watch, tacks on set-top-box rental fees, and often locks you into a contract, all while the monthly price keeps drifting upward. We covered the latest round of those hikes in our look at [streaming and cable price increases in 2026](/news/streaming-cable-price-increases-2026).

Cutting the cord flips that model. Instead of paying for a giant bundle to get the handful of things you want, you assemble exactly what you watch — and you can change it whenever you like. The trade-off is that you become your own TV package manager, which is why it helps to understand your options before you cancel anything.

## What You Keep — and What You Have to Replace

Before you cut the cord, it's worth being clear about what cable actually gives you, so you can replace each piece deliberately:

- **Local channels** (CBC, CTV, Global, City, and your regional stations) — easily replaced, often for free.
- **Live sports** — the trickiest piece, because rights are scattered across services. More on this below.
- **Live news** — widely available through streaming apps and 24-hour news channels carried on live-TV services.
- **On-demand shows and movies** — this is what streaming does best; you're spoiled for choice here.

Once you can see the four buckets, "replacing cable" stops feeling like one huge decision and becomes four small, manageable ones.

## How to Watch Live TV Without Cable in Canada

Here are the four real routes, from simplest to most complete. Most Canadians end up using one or two of them together.

### 1. A Free Over-the-Air Antenna

If you live in or near a city, a one-time **HDTV antenna** (roughly $30–$60) pulls in your local channels — CBC, CTV, Global, and more — in crisp, uncompressed HD, completely free, forever. There's no subscription and no internet needed. The catch is range: the farther you are from the broadcast towers, the more your reception depends on antenna quality and placement. For local news and major networks, though, it's the cheapest win in cord-cutting.

### 2. Individual Streaming Apps

Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+, Crave, and the rest are the obvious on-demand layer. They're excellent for shows and movies, but they aren't built for *live* TV, and the costs stack up fast — three or four apps quietly add up to a cable-sized bill. They're a great supplement, not a full cable replacement on their own.

### 3. Live TV Streaming Services

These stream real, live channels over the internet — think of them as cable delivered through an app instead of a box. You get a channel guide, live news, and often some sports, without any hardware rental. They're a solid middle ground, though the channel lineup and price vary, and premium sports frequently cost extra.

### 4. All-in-One IPTV

**IPTV** (Internet Protocol Television) delivers live TV, sports, and on-demand content through a single app over your existing internet connection. Instead of an antenna *plus* three streaming apps *plus* a separate sports service, everything lives in one place, on the devices you already own, for one flat fee. For people who mainly cut cable to escape the juggling and the surcharges, this is the closest single replacement — which is exactly what [Easy IPTV](/#pricing) is built to be.

## What You Need to Get Started

The barrier to entry is refreshingly low. You need two things:

**A device.** Any TV works. If it's already a smart TV, you may not need anything else. If it isn't, a small streaming device — a **Fire TV Stick** or an **Android TV box**, usually $30–$70 — plugs into an HDMI port and turns any television into a streaming machine. You can also watch on a phone, tablet, or laptop. Our [installation guide](/installation-guide) walks through setting up Easy IPTV on each of these in a few minutes.

**A decent internet connection.** A single live stream needs surprisingly little: roughly **10 Mbps for stable HD** and about **25 Mbps for 4K**. Most Canadian home plans clear that with room to spare, even with a few people watching at once — you can confirm yours with a quick [Speedtest by Ookla](https://www.speedtest.net/). If your picture ever stutters despite a fast plan, the cause is usually routing or the device rather than raw speed; our guide to [fixing IPTV buffering on Firestick](/blog/fix-iptv-buffering-on-firestick) covers exactly that.

## How to Watch Live Sports Without Cable

Sports is the reason a lot of people hesitate to cut the cord — and fairly so, because live sports rights in Canada are deliberately split across services. No single mainstream app carries everything, so following more than one team or league can mean stacking two or three separate subscriptions just to see the games you care about. That scattering is a real headache, and it's only grown as broadcast deals have shifted; we broke down one big example in our piece on [watching the NHL without cable](/news/watch-nhl-cord-cutting-canada-2026).

This is where an all-in-one service earns its keep. Because IPTV bundles the live sports channels alongside everything else, you keep game day in the same app as your regular TV, instead of hopping between apps and remembering which subscription has tonight's match. If sports is the main thing tying you to cable, consolidating it into one lineup is usually the difference between cord-cutting that sticks and cord-cutting you quietly abandon.

## Is Watching Live TV Without Cable Legal in Canada?

Yes. Cutting the cord is completely legal — an antenna, a licensed streaming service, and a paid IPTV subscription are all legitimate ways to watch. The only thing to steer clear of is a service that seems too good to be true: a "free" app promising every premium channel and sports package for nothing, with no company behind it and no real support. A trustworthy service is upfront about what it includes, charges a fair and clearly stated price, offers help when you need it, and usually lets you test it first. If you can't tell who you're paying or what you're getting, that's your signal to walk away.
